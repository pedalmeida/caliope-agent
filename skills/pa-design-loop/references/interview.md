# Step 3: the "this or that" interview

Pedro is visual and answers fast when he sees two real things side by side. Adjectives ("premium", "clean") mean nothing until they are attached to a picture.

## Build the pairs

- 5 to 7 pairs. More tires him and adds noise.
- One design axis per pair. Both sides should be equally good work, differing mainly on that axis, so the pick tells you something.
- Real images from the reference cards (Mobbin, Dribbble, Site Dump, Inspo), never generated mockups.
- Each side gets a two-to-five-word label of what differs ("airy, 4 KPIs" vs "dense, 8 KPIs").
- Put any reference Pedro already sent on one side of the most relevant pair, to confirm what he liked about it.

Default axes for app and dashboard UI (pick the ones still open after his brief):

| Axis | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Theme | light, white cards on grey | dark |
| Density | 4 KPIs, lots of air | many numbers, compact |
| Colour | neutral plus one accent | several category colours |
| Personality | personality in avatars/illustration only | branded everywhere |
| Data | numbers with context lines | charts and sparklines |
| Navigation | sidebar | top tabs |
| Cards | hairline borders, no shadow | soft shadows, bigger radius |
| Type | geometric sans | grotesk plus mono for data |

Sites and brand work swap in their own axes (photography vs illustration, editorial vs product-led, serif vs sans).

## Deliver it

Publish one page (Artifact) with the pairs numbered, both images per pair at a size where detail reads, the label under each, and a one-line question per pair. Below the pairs: "Anything you never want to see?". Pedro answers in chat or Slack in one line, e.g. `1A 2A 3B 4neither 5A, never: emoji, neon`. Accept answers in any form; do not make him fill a form.

If he says "neither", ask one follow-up for that pair only: what both got wrong.

## Record

`interview.md`: each pair (axis, A and B with source links), his pick verbatim, the never-list verbatim, and one line per pair on what the pick means for the design. That last line is what step 4 turns into checks.
